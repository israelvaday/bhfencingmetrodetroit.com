Roofing Grosse Pointe Woods homeowners rely on

Grosse Pointe Woods is a neighborhood of substantial, well-kept homes — stately Colonials, brick Tudors and mid-century two-stories set on tree-lined streets a short distance from Lake St. Clair. Much of the housing stock went up between the 1940s and 1960s, which means a lot of these roofs are already on their second or third covering. The steep pitches, dormers, bay windows and cut-up rooflines look beautiful, but they also create extra valleys, flashing points and transitions — exactly the spots where an older roof tends to leak first.

Sitting this close to the water, roofs in the Woods take a beating that inland neighborhoods don't. Lake-effect moisture keeps north-facing slopes damp and feeds moss and algae; spring storms roll in off the lake with wind and hail that lift and bruise shingles; and Michigan's freeze-thaw winters pack ice into the eaves, where ice dams back water up under the shingles and into the ceilings below. Add heavy February snow load and the mature trees that drop limbs and clog valleys, and it's easy to see why roofs here often age faster than their shingles' rated life.

Homes like these deserve materials and workmanship that match them. That's why so many owners in the Woods choose premium architectural asphalt, synthetic cedar-look shake or standing-seam metal — roofs that hold their curb appeal and shrug off lake storms — installed over a proper ice-and-water shield, with the attic ventilation these homes need to keep ice dams from forming in the first place.

The roofing Grosse Pointe Woods homes need most

For most Grosse Pointe Woods homeowners, the work that matters most is full roof replacement on aging mid-century roofs and the premium installs these homes call for — architectural shingles, cedar-look synthetic shake and standing-seam metal — along with targeted leak repair around chimneys, dormers, skylights and valley flashing, where water tends to sneak in on complex rooflines. Because ice dams are such a common problem this close to the lake, we pay close attention to eave protection, attic ventilation and insulation as part of any replacement, and we handle the storm-damage repairs, shingle and flashing fixes, and gutter work that keep a good roof watertight between now and its next replacement.

Roofing in Grosse Pointe Woods — FAQs

Do you serve Grosse Pointe Woods?

Yes. Grosse Pointe Woods sits right in our service area. We're a Metro Detroit roofing company covering the Grosse Pointes and surrounding suburbs within about 35 miles across Wayne, Oakland and Macomb counties. Send us your address through our free estimate request and we'll set up a time to take a look at your roof.

A storm just hit and my roof is leaking — how fast can you come out?

Active leaks and storm damage go to the top of our list. When our schedule allows we can often get out the same day or the next business day to assess the damage and get a temporary cover in place so it doesn't spread. Our hours are Sunday through Thursday 9am-5pm and Friday 9am-noon (closed Saturday); reach out through our free inspection request and we'll get you on the calendar as fast as we can. We don't advertise 24/7 service — we'd rather be straight about our hours than over-promise.

What does a new roof cost in Grosse Pointe Woods?

Every roof is different, so treat these as typical ranges rather than a quote. For many homes here, a full architectural asphalt shingle replacement lands somewhere in the $9,000–$20,000 range, depending on size, pitch and how cut-up the roofline is. Premium materials like standing-seam metal or synthetic cedar shake run higher. The only way to know your real number is a free on-site inspection and written estimate — which we're glad to provide.

Can you help with an insurance claim after hail or wind damage?

We can. We'll inspect the roof, document the storm damage with photos and notes, and walk the findings through with you and your insurance adjuster so nothing gets missed. Keep in mind that you're responsible for your policy's deductible — anyone who offers to 'waive' or cover it isn't playing straight, and in Michigan that's insurance fraud. What we can do is make sure the damage is documented properly and the repair is done right.
