Siding Built for Michigan's Punishing Seasons

Metro Detroit siding takes a beating no catalog photo ever shows. Freeze-thaw cycles pry at seams, ice dams and lake-effect moisture work their way behind loose panels, spring wind and hail crack and dent the surface, and humid summers invite mold and rot on the shaded north side. Good siding does three jobs at once: it keeps water and wind out of your walls, it holds heat in through a long Michigan winter, and it makes your home look cared-for. When we install siding across Wayne, Oakland, and Macomb counties, we build for all three—not just curb appeal.

  • Weather-tight panels and proper flashing that shed wind-driven rain and blowing snow
  • Insulated options that cut drafts and take the edge off winter heating bills
  • Moisture management behind the siding so freeze-thaw and lake-effect humidity can't rot your sheathing
  • Fade- and impact-resistant materials that hold their color and shape through hail season
  • A finished look that lifts your whole home's curb appeal

Vinyl, Insulated Vinyl & Fiber-Cement: Choosing the Right Siding

There's no single "best" siding—only the best fit for your home, your budget, and how long you plan to stay. We'll walk your exterior with you, talk through the trade-offs in plain language, and never push you toward the priciest option just because it's the priciest. Here's the short version of what most metro Detroit homeowners are choosing between:

Whatever you land on, we install to the manufacturer's spec so your material warranty stays intact, and we back our own labor with a workmanship guarantee. Not sure which way to go? That's exactly what a free estimate is for.

  • Vinyl siding — the budget-friendly workhorse. Low maintenance, never needs painting, and available in dozens of colors and profiles. A solid choice for most homes.
  • Insulated vinyl — vinyl with a rigid foam backing bonded to the panel. It adds R-value, resists dents better, and lies flatter on the wall, which pays off in comfort and energy savings through a long Michigan winter.
  • Fiber-cement (the James Hardie-style boards) — a heavier, premium product that mimics real wood grain and resists fire, rot, and impact while holding up beautifully to freeze-thaw. It costs more up front and takes longer to hang, but it's about as durable as siding gets.

Signs Your Siding Is Ready to Be Replaced

Siding rarely fails all at once. It gives you warning signs first—and catching them early can be the difference between a straightforward panel swap and repairing water-damaged sheathing underneath. If you're seeing any of these, it's worth having us take a look:

Not every one of these means a full replacement—sometimes a targeted repair buys you several more years. We'll tell you honestly which camp your home is in.

  • Cracks, splits, or holes—often left by hail or flying debris after a spring storm
  • Panels that are warping, buckling, or pulling away from the wall
  • Chalky, badly faded color that no longer matches from one side of the house to the other
  • Soft, spongy, or visibly rotting spots, especially low on the wall or on the shaded north face
  • Peeling paint or wallpaper inside the home, a hint that moisture is getting through
  • Heating and cooling bills creeping up as old siding and insulation lose their grip
  • Mold, mildew, or moss taking hold in the seams

Trim, Soffit, Fascia & Storm Damage

Siding is only as good as the details around it. The soffit (the underside of your roof overhang) and the fascia (the board your gutters hang from) are what keep your attic ventilated and water out of your roof edge—and in Michigan they're prime targets for ice dams, wind, and woodpeckers. We handle the whole exterior envelope, not just the flat walls.

After a wind or hail storm, we'll document any damage thoroughly with photos and measurements, and if you decide to file an insurance claim we're glad to meet your adjuster on-site and walk them through what we found. To be clear about how that works: your insurer decides what they'll cover, and you're responsible for your policy's deductible—we don't discount, waive, or absorb it, and anyone who promises to is setting you up for trouble. What we do is make sure nothing legitimate gets missed.

  • Soffit and fascia replacement to protect your roof edge and attic ventilation
  • New trim around windows, doors, and corners for a clean, weather-tight finish
  • Wrapping of exposed wood in aluminum or PVC so it never rots or needs painting again
  • Storm and hail damage assessment with clear, organized photo documentation
  • On-site help walking your insurance adjuster through the damage we've documented

Siding and a New Roof: Better Together

A lot of our siding jobs start as roofing calls—and for good reason. If your roof and siding are close in age, they're often wearing out on the same timeline, and tackling both together saves you money and hassle. You share one setup, one crew, and one round of cleanup, and you end up with a home that looks and performs like new all at once instead of in mismatched phases. It's also the smartest moment to correct the flashing and water-management details where the roof and walls meet—the exact spots where Michigan leaks love to start.

  • One mobilization and one cleanup instead of two separate projects
  • Coordinated flashing and water management where the roof meets the wall
  • A consistent, finished look across your whole exterior
  • Often more cost-effective than scheduling the two jobs years apart

Siding — FAQs

How much does siding installation cost in metro Detroit?

Every home is different, but as a typical range, vinyl siding often runs about $6,000–$14,000 for an average Detroit-area home, while insulated vinyl and fiber-cement usually land higher given the material and labor. House size, trim work, and how much old siding has to come off all shift the number. We'll give you a free, exact estimate—no guessing.

How long does a siding replacement take?

Most single-family homes in the metro Detroit area take somewhere between two and five working days, depending on the size of the house, the material you choose, and the weather. Fiber-cement runs longer than vinyl because it's heavier and slower to cut and hang. If we find rot or damaged sheathing underneath, we'll flag it before adding any time.

Can you repair just a section of siding instead of replacing it all?

Often, yes. If the damage is limited—say, a few cracked panels after a hailstorm—a targeted repair can save you real money. The one catch is color: older siding fades, so a patch may not match perfectly. We'll show you both options honestly and let you decide what makes sense for your home and budget.

My siding was damaged in a storm—will insurance pay for it?

That's between you and your insurer, but we can help. We document storm and hail damage with detailed photos and measurements and will meet your adjuster on-site to walk them through it. Keep in mind you're responsible for your policy's deductible—we can't waive or cover it—but we make sure legitimate damage doesn't go unnoticed.

Does insulated siding really lower my energy bills?

It helps. Insulated vinyl has rigid foam bonded to the back that adds R-value and reduces the drafts that drive up heating costs through a Michigan winter. It won't replace attic or wall insulation, and savings vary by home, but many homeowners notice a more comfortable, less drafty house—and a bit of relief on the bills.

Should I replace my roof and siding at the same time?

If both are aging, it's often worth it. Doing them together means one crew, one cleanup, and one chance to get the flashing where your roof meets your walls exactly right—a common leak point in Michigan. It can also cost less than scheduling the two jobs years apart. We're happy to look at both during your free estimate.
