When Metro Detroit Homeowners Need a Roof Inspection

A roof inspection isn't only for when water's already dripping through the ceiling. The smartest time to look is before a small, cheap-to-fix problem turns into a soaked deck and a stained ceiling. Homeowners across Wayne, Oakland, and Macomb counties book an inspection for a handful of common reasons.

Whatever brought you here, the visit starts the same way — a careful look and an honest answer, in writing.

  • Free estimate visits — you suspect a problem, or a roofer knocked on your door, and you want a straight second opinion before spending a dime.
  • Annual and seasonal checkups — a yearly once-over, ideally each spring, to catch winter wear before storm season arrives.
  • Pre-purchase and home-sale inspections — buying or selling a home and needing a clear read on the true condition of the roof.
  • Post-storm inspections — after a wind, hail, or heavy-snow event, with documented photos to support an insurance claim if you decide to file one.

What We Check, From Ridge to Attic

A real inspection goes well beyond a glance from the driveway. We get up top and into the details, because most roof failures start small and out of sight — a lifted shingle, a cracked boot, a bead of failed sealant around a vent. Here's where we focus.

Put together, these findings tell us not just whether your roof leaks today, but how much life it has left.

  • Shingles — cracking, curling, blistering, granule loss, and any that are lifted or missing after high winds.
  • Flashing — the metal at chimneys, walls, and skylights, where a surprising share of leaks actually begin.
  • Valleys — the channels where two roof planes meet and shed the most rain and snowmelt.
  • Penetrations and boots — the rubber seals around plumbing vents and pipes that dry out, crack, and leak with age.
  • Ventilation — intake and exhaust airflow that, when blocked, bakes shingles in summer and feeds ice dams in winter.
  • Gutters and drainage — clogs, sagging, and the tell-tale wear that points to ice-dam trouble.
  • The attic — the honest truth-teller, where we look for active leaks, water stains, damp decking, and thin or matted insulation.

Why Annual Inspections Pay Off in Michigan's Freeze-Thaw Climate

Michigan asks a lot of a roof. Water works into a small gap, freezes overnight, expands, and pries that gap a little wider — then thaws and does it all again, sometimes dozens of times in a single winter. That freeze-thaw cycle is how minor wear quietly becomes a real leak. Add in the rest of our weather and the case for a yearly inspection makes itself.

A once-a-year inspection catches this wear while it's still a small repair — and consistently gets more usable years out of the roof you already paid for.

  • Ice dams that back meltwater up under shingles and into the attic.
  • Heavy snow load that stresses the deck and flashing over a long winter.
  • Spring wind and hail storms that lift, bruise, and crack shingles.
  • Lake-effect moisture and humid summers that feed rot, mold, and premature aging.

Roof Inspections — FAQs

How much does a roof inspection cost in metro Detroit?

Many BH Roofing inspections are free — they come as part of a no-obligation estimate for repair or replacement work. For a standalone report, such as a pre-purchase or home-sale inspection, typical metro Detroit pricing runs roughly $150 to $400, though every roof is different. Either way, we'll confirm exact pricing up front, and your estimate is always free.

How long does a roof inspection take?

Most residential roof inspections take about 45 minutes to two hours, depending on the size, pitch, and complexity of your roof and whether we access the attic. Larger homes or steep, cut-up rooflines take a little longer. We won't rush it — a thorough look is the whole point — and you'll get your written assessment with photos shortly after.

How often should I have my roof inspected in Michigan?

In Michigan, we recommend a professional roof inspection once a year, ideally in spring after the freeze-thaw season, plus a check after any major wind, hail, or heavy-snow event. Our climate is hard on roofs — ice dams, thawing and refreezing, and lake-effect moisture all take a toll — so catching small issues early keeps them from becoming expensive leaks.

Do I need to be home for the inspection?

You don't have to be home for the exterior portion of the inspection, but we'll need access if you'd like us to check the attic, which is where hidden leaks, moisture, and insulation problems often show up first. Many homeowners like to be there so we can walk the findings and answer questions face to face.

Will you try to sell me a full roof replacement?

No. Our job is to tell you what your roof actually needs — not to talk you into a replacement you don't. If a repair or some maintenance will get you several more good years, we'll say so. If the roof is truly at the end of its life, we'll show you the photos and explain why, then leave the decision to you.

Can you inspect my roof for storm or hail damage?

After a storm, yes. We inspect for wind and hail damage and document what we find with clear, labeled photos you can share with your insurance company. We can walk your adjuster through the damage, but keep in mind the claim and your deductible are handled between you and your insurer — we're honest about that from the start.
