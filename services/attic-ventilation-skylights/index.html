If your roof grows a wall of ice along the eaves every January, or your ceilings show brown stains after each freeze-thaw swing, your attic ventilation is almost certainly out of balance. In Michigan's climate, warm attic air and cold eaves are a recipe for ice dams, condensation, and shingles that age out years early. We diagnose the whole system — intake, exhaust, insulation, and flashing — and fix the cause, not just the symptom.

Why Michigan Winters Are So Hard on an Under-Ventilated Roof

Metro Detroit roofs take a beating that milder climates never see: heavy snow load, weeks of lake-effect moisture, and freeze-thaw cycles that can swing above and below freezing in a single day. When an attic isn't vented properly, heat leaking up from your living space warms the roof deck. Snow melts near the ridge, trickles down to the cold overhangs, and refreezes into an ice dam that traps meltwater behind it — and that trapped water works its way under your shingles and into the house.

The same trapped attic heat and moisture do quieter damage year-round. Warm, humid air hitting a cold roof deck condenses into frost and drips, which soaks insulation, feeds mold and mildew, and rots sheathing. It also cooks your shingles from underneath in summer, so they curl, blister, and lose granules long before they should. Good ventilation is one of the cheapest ways to protect an expensive roof.

  • Ice dams that back water up under shingles and stain ceilings and walls
  • Attic condensation, frost, and the mold and wood rot that follow
  • Soggy, matted insulation that stops doing its job
  • Premature shingle aging — curling, blistering, and granule loss
  • Higher heating and cooling bills from an attic that can't breathe

Balanced Ventilation Done Right: Ridge Vents, Soffit Vents & Airflow

A healthy attic breathes from bottom to top. Cool outside air enters low at the soffits (intake) and rises out through the ridge (exhaust), carrying heat and moisture with it. The key word is balanced — intake and exhaust have to be matched. We've walked plenty of Detroit-area attics where someone added a ridge vent but the soffits were painted shut or stuffed with insulation, so the roof was quietly pulling conditioned air out of the house instead of venting the attic.

We look at the whole picture and size the system to your roof, then keep the airflow path clear from soffit to ridge so it actually works in a Michigan winter.

  • Continuous ridge vent paired with adequate, unblocked soffit intake
  • Baffles (rafter vents) that keep insulation from choking off the soffits
  • Correcting mismatched or competing vents that short-circuit airflow
  • Gable, box, or powered venting where the roof design calls for it
  • Insulation and air-sealing review, since ventilation and insulation work as a team

Ice Dams: Prevention First, Safe Removal When You Need It

The real cure for ice dams is keeping the roof deck cold and even — that means balanced ventilation, enough attic insulation to stop heat loss, and a self-adhering ice-and-water shield membrane along the eaves and valleys during a re-roof. Michigan code and common sense both point to that membrane at the vulnerable edges, and it's your last line of defense when the weather turns brutal.

When a dam has already formed, please don't climb up and chip at it with a hammer or shovel — that's how shingles crack, gutters bend, and people get hurt. Roof rakes and calcium-chloride pucks help from the ground. For a dam that's already leaking, low-pressure steam is the safe way to melt it without harming the roof. When our schedule allows, we can get out to help.

  • Ice-and-water shield along eaves and valleys during any re-roof
  • Attic insulation and air-sealing to cut the heat loss that starts dams
  • Balanced venting to keep the whole deck at one cold temperature
  • Safe, low-pressure steam removal for dams that are actively leaking
  • Honest guidance on what to handle from the ground versus leave to us

Skylights: Installation, Replacement & Fixing Old Leaks

A skylight brings real daylight into a dark hallway, bath, or stairwell, but it's also a hole in your roof — and in this climate, the flashing around it is where trouble starts. Curb-mount and deck-mount skylights each have their place, and for interior rooms with no straight line to the roof, a sun tunnel (tubular skylight) pipes daylight down through a reflective shaft without a full framed opening.

Most skylight leaks aren't the glass — they're worn flashing, failed seals, or a unit that has simply aged past its prime after years of freeze-thaw. If you're already re-roofing, that's the smart moment to replace or fully re-flash any skylight, because the surrounding roof is open and the labor overlaps. Doing it later as a standalone repair almost always costs more and risks disturbing new shingles.

  • New skylight installation — curb-mount and deck-mount options
  • Replacing old, fogged, or leak-prone units with modern flashed models
  • Sun tunnels to daylight interior rooms with no direct roof access
  • Re-flashing and resealing existing skylights during a re-roof
  • Diagnosing whether that ceiling stain is the skylight or the roof around it

Ventilation & Skylights — FAQs

How much does fixing attic ventilation or ice-dam issues cost in metro Detroit?

Every roof is different, but typical metro Detroit ranges run roughly $600 to $2,000 to add balanced ridge-and-soffit venting, and about $300 to $600-plus for professional steam ice-dam removal. Skylight and insulation work vary more widely. These are ballpark ranges, not quotes — request a free inspection and we'll measure your roof and give you an exact price.

What actually causes ice dams on my roof?

Ice dams form when heat escaping your living space warms the attic and roof deck, melting snow up near the ridge. That meltwater runs down to the cold eaves and overhangs, refreezes, and builds a ridge of ice. Water pools behind that ridge, backs up under the shingles, and finds its way into your ceilings and walls.

Can better ventilation really stop ice dams?

Ventilation is a big part of it. Balanced intake and exhaust keeps your attic close to the outdoor temperature, so snow melts evenly instead of refreezing at the eaves. Paired with solid attic insulation and an ice-and-water shield along the eaves, it treats the root cause. Chipping ice off every winter only manages the symptom.

Should I replace my old skylight during a re-roof?

Usually, yes. Skylights and their flashing wear out on roughly the same timeline as shingles, and older units are a common leak source after years of Michigan freeze-thaw. Replacing or fully re-flashing a skylight while the surrounding roof is already open costs far less than a separate trip later, and it protects your new roof.

Is it safe to remove ice dams myself?

We don't recommend climbing an icy roof or hacking at ice with hammers or picks — you can crack shingles, wreck gutters, and get badly hurt. A roof rake and calcium-chloride pucks help from the ground, but a dam that's already leaking calls for low-pressure steam, which melts ice without harming the roof. When our schedule allows, we can help.
