Tired of replacing asphalt shingles every couple of decades — or watching ice dams chew up your eaves every February? A metal roof is the long-game answer. Engineered to shed heavy snow, resist wind and fire, and hold up through Michigan's freeze-thaw punishment, it's the last roof many homes ever need.

Why a Metal Roof Is Built for Michigan Weather

Michigan asks a lot of a roof. Deep freeze-thaw cycles pry at seams, ice dams back water up under shingles, spring storms hurl wind and hail, and lake-effect moisture never really lets up. Metal handles all of it. A properly installed standing-seam or metal-shingle roof is one of the toughest, longest-lasting systems you can put over your home — and it's engineered for exactly the conditions we see across Wayne, Oakland, and Macomb counties.

  • 40-70 year lifespan — often two to three times longer than asphalt
  • Sheds heavy snow before it loads up your roof and gutters
  • Smooth panels give ice dams far less to grab onto
  • Reflective 'cool-roof' finishes bounce summer heat and can trim cooling bills
  • Class A fire resistance and wind ratings that stand up to spring storms
  • Won't crack, curl, or rot the way aging asphalt eventually does

Standing-Seam vs. Metal Shingle: Two Ways to Go

Metal roofing isn't one look anymore. Two main systems cover most of the homes we work on, and both come in a wide range of colors and finishes to match your house.

Standing-seam uses long vertical panels with raised, interlocking seams and concealed fasteners — clean, modern, and equally at home on a contemporary build or a farmhouse-style roof. Stone-coated metal shingles are pressed and textured to mimic slate, cedar shake, or dimensional asphalt, so you get metal's durability with a traditional profile that blends right into an established metro Detroit neighborhood.

  • Standing-seam: sleek vertical lines, hidden fasteners, architectural feel
  • Metal shingle / stone-coated: slate, shake, or shingle-look textures
  • Dozens of colors, from classic charcoal and bronze to bolder accents
  • Matte, low-gloss, and reflective 'cool-roof' finishes available
  • Both systems carry manufacturer-backed material warranties

The Investment: Higher Upfront Cost, Real Long-Term Value

There's no way around it — metal costs more upfront than asphalt. But the comparison isn't fair unless you look at the whole timeline. Asphalt may need replacing two or three times before a single metal roof reaches the end of its life, and metal holds its performance the whole way through instead of slowly degrading season after season.

Typical metro Detroit metal roofs run roughly $12,000 to $35,000, depending on the size and pitch of your roof and whether you choose standing-seam or metal shingle. Every roof is different, though — the only real number is the one we put on paper after seeing your home. Metal tends to make the most sense if you plan to stay put, want the lowest lifetime cost, or simply never want to think about your roof again.

  • Higher upfront cost than asphalt, lower cost spread across its lifespan
  • Can add curb appeal and resale value to your home
  • Reflective 'cool-roof' colors can help with summer cooling costs
  • Best fit for long-term homeowners and forever-homes
  • Typical ranges only — every roof is different, so we quote yours exactly

Why Professional Installation Makes or Breaks a Metal Roof

A metal roof is only as good as the crew that installs it. Metal expands and contracts with temperature swings — and Michigan gives it a big swing between a January cold snap and a humid July afternoon. If panels are fastened too tight, seams are sloppy, or the wrong underlayment goes down, you can end up with oil-canning, loosened fasteners, or leaks that undo the entire point of going metal.

We install for the long haul: the right high-temperature synthetic underlayment, proper ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys, fastening that lets the metal move the way it's designed to, and clean detailing around every chimney, vent, and valley. Done right, it's a roof you can forget about for decades.

  • Fastening and clips that allow for panel expansion and contraction
  • High-temperature synthetic underlayment rated for metal systems
  • Ice-and-water shield at eaves, valleys, and vulnerable areas
  • Precise flashing and detailing around every roof penetration
  • Balanced attic ventilation to protect the whole assembly

Metal Roofing — FAQs

How long does a metal roof last in Michigan?

A quality metal roof typically lasts 40 to 70 years — often two to three times longer than asphalt. Metal shrugs off freeze-thaw cycles, heavy snow load, and lake-effect moisture that wear other roofs down fast. For many metro Detroit homeowners, it's genuinely the last roof they'll ever buy. We'll assess your exact home during a free inspection.

How much does metal roofing cost in metro Detroit?

Every roof is different, but typical metro Detroit metal roofs run roughly $12,000 to $35,000 depending on size, pitch, and whether you choose standing-seam or metal shingle. That's more upfront than asphalt, but spread across a 40-to-70-year lifespan, the cost per year often comes out lower. The only way to know your number is a free, no-obligation estimate.

Standing-seam or metal shingle — which should I choose?

Both are excellent; it comes down to look and budget. Standing-seam gives clean vertical lines and hidden fasteners — a modern, architectural feel. Stone-coated metal shingles mimic the texture of slate, wood shake, or dimensional asphalt, so they blend into traditional neighborhoods. We'll walk you through samples and colors during your free estimate and help you match your home.

Will a metal roof be noisy when it rains or hails?

It's a common worry, but no. A metal roof isn't a bare barn panel — it's installed over solid decking and a synthetic underlayment, with insulation and your attic beneath it. Once it's on, rain and hail sound about the same as they do under asphalt. Many homeowners never notice a difference at all.

Does a metal roof really help with ice dams and snow?

Yes — that's one of its biggest Michigan advantages. Metal's slick surface sheds snow before it can build into heavy loads, and its smooth panels give ice far less to grip, which reduces the ice-dam problems that plague asphalt eaves. Paired with proper attic ventilation and ice-and-water shield, it's one of the best defenses against our freeze-thaw winters.
