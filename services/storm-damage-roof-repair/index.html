Wind, Hail & Debris: What Storm Damage Looks Like on a Michigan Roof

Not all storm damage is obvious. After a big blow you might see shingles in the yard, but plenty of damage hides in plain sight — creased tabs that will curl and leak later, or hail bruises that knock the protective granules loose and shorten the roof's life. Left alone, small hits turn into active leaks once the next freeze-thaw cycle or spring rain works its way in.

That's why it pays to have a roofer look closely after any serious storm, even when the roof looks fine from the driveway. Here's what we're checking for:

  • Wind-lifted or creased shingles that have broken their seal and flap in the next gust
  • Missing shingles and exposed underlayment after straight-line winds
  • Hail bruising — soft spots and knocked-off granules that expose the asphalt mat
  • Debris impact from branches, plus dented or torn metal flashing, vents, and gutters
  • Backed-out or popped nails that let water track underneath the shingles
  • Interior clues: fresh ceiling stains, daylight in the attic, or grit collecting in the downspouts

Honest Help With Your Insurance Claim

If your damage warrants a claim, we'll make the process easier — and we'll always be straight with you about how it works. We document the damage thoroughly and can meet your insurance adjuster on-site to walk the roof together, so nothing legitimate gets missed.

What we won't do is bend the rules. Your deductible is your responsibility, and any contractor promising a "free roof" by waiving, covering, or "eating" it is asking you to take part in insurance fraud, which is illegal in Michigan. We'd rather earn your trust with honest work than make a promise that could put you at risk.

  • We photograph and document all storm damage for your claim file
  • We can meet your adjuster on-site to review the roof together
  • We provide a clear, itemized estimate of the repair or replacement scope
  • You pay your own deductible — we never waive, discount, or cover it, and we won't pretend the roof is free
  • If the damage is minor, we'll tell you honestly so you can weigh a claim against a simple repair

Storm & Hail Damage — FAQs

How much does storm damage roof repair cost in metro Detroit?

It depends on the damage. Minor repairs — resealing a few wind-lifted shingles or patching a small section — often run a few hundred dollars, while larger repairs typically fall in the $600 to $2,500 range. Full storm-driven replacements cost more. Every roof is different, so we'll give you a free, exact estimate after inspecting yours.

How fast can you tarp or repair my roof after a storm?

When your schedule and ours line up, we can sometimes tarp an exposed roof the same day to stop water from getting in — always during our business hours. Permanent repairs are usually scheduled within a few days, depending on materials and storm volume. Reach out and we'll be honest about exactly what we can do and when.

Do you work with my insurance company on a storm claim?

Yes. We document your storm damage with photos and a written scope, and we can meet your adjuster on-site to review the roof together so nothing legitimate gets overlooked. To be clear, though: your deductible is your responsibility. We never waive or cover it, and we won't promise a "free" insurance roof — that would be fraud.

How do I know if I have hail or wind damage I can't see?

A lot of storm damage isn't visible from the ground. Hail can bruise shingles and knock off protective granules, and wind can crease tabs that look fine but will leak later. Warning signs include grit in your gutters, fresh ceiling stains, or shingle pieces in the yard. A free inspection is the only sure way to know.

Should I file an insurance claim or just pay for the repair myself?

It depends on how much damage you have and the size of your deductible. If the repair costs less than your deductible, paying out of pocket usually makes more sense. For widespread wind or hail damage, a claim may be worth it. We'll inspect, show you the damage honestly, and help you weigh both options — no pressure.
